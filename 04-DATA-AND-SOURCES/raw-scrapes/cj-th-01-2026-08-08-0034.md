## FINDING 5: FMT Opinion Piece — "Could the Law Prevent Accountability in the TH Fiasco?"

**Source:** Free Malaysia Today (FMT) — opinion column by Walter Sandosam
**Date:** 2026-08-07
**URL:** https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2026/08/07/could-the-law-prevent-accountability-in-the-th-fiasco/
**MP Named:** None — opinion piece by non-MP analyst. Walter Sandosam is former president of Institute of Internal Auditors, Malaysia.
**Summary:** The most detailed examination of the legal accountability barrier in the TH fiasco. Key findings:
1. **Tabung Haji Act 1995 gives the minister "unparalleled authority"** — appointing, reshuffling, terminating board members; by default, controlling senior management including the CEO.
2. **Minister invoked Section 6(5) of the TH Act** to terminate then-chairman Md Nor Yusof and then-CEO Nik Hasyudeen Yusoff months before their terms ended — WITHOUT giving reasons.
3. **Charging someone with "abuse of power" under Section 23 of the MACC Act faces an uphill battle** because the TH Act grants the minister the authority to act. Proving malice or ill-intent is the biggest hurdle.
4. **"One cannot be prosecuted for being either ill-advised, ill-informed or outright stupid! Malice or ill-intent has to be proven."**
5. **6 matters were referred to MACC** — allegations included misrepresentation, concealment of information, corruption, abuse of power, forgery, manipulation of investment reports. **No court charges resulted from the AGC.**
6. Internal TH investigations led to unverified "punishments": demotions and "severe warnings."
7. MACC's special task force to reinvestigate is called "superfluous" — "after the horses have bolted."
8. Similar ministerial authority issues seen in HRD Corporation and historically in the PKFZ case.
9. Author argues: "Legislations granting 'unlimited' powers to ministers create accountability dilemmas, as ministers are inevitably politicians."
10. Remedial progress since 2018: TH has undertaken significant remedial work, proper governance frameworks put in place, institution described as "stronger."
11. Historical personalities named: **Nik Hasyudeen Yusoff** (former TH CEO), **Md Nor Yusof** (former TH chairman) — both terminated under Section 6(5) without reasons.

**ANALYSIS:** This is the most specific legislative-reform framing to date — identifies Section 6(5) and Section 23 MACC Act as the core legal barrier. DIRECTLY relevant to PIR-TH-06 (legislative amendments) and PIR-TH-02 (enforcement). While NOT an MP statement, this analysis provides the intellectual framework that any MP could use on the debate floor to argue that the TH Act itself shields wrongdoers and must be amended. No MP has yet referenced Section 6(5) or the Section 23 MACC Act hurdle.
**PIR Mapping:** PIR-TH-06 (legislative amendments — Section 6(5) ministerial powers identified); PIR-TH-02 (enforcement — Section 23 MACC Act hurdle analyzed); PIR-TH-01 (accountability — legal barrier to holding individuals accountable)
**Confidence:** HIGH — FMT opinion piece with specific legal provisions, named individuals, and historical analysis. T3 (analytical interpretation) — not a factual claim but legal analysis.
**Tag:** legal-analysis, th-act-section-6, section-23-macc-act, accountability-barrier, legislative-reform-framework, non-mp-analysis
